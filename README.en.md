# Japan in 2100 (town-2100)

[日本語](README.md)

A map that shows Japan's population up to 2100. You drill down from Japan to a prefecture to a town and see each town's population, its age structure, and whether that population is above or below each facility's "population guideline". You can also reallocate people between regions and see what changes. An AI calls the app's own calculations as tools and answers from their results.

The app does not recommend what to keep or give up. It shows facts so people can decide.

The author's view (作者の見解): the author thinks that if every rural region in Japan were revitalised at the same time, it would not add up in terms of population. The app is not a proof of that view; it lays out material for checking it. (This sentence was written by Claude from the author's remarks and confirmed by the author on 6 November 2026.)

## Try it in 3 minutes (for judges)

Open the public page, https://japan2100.pages.dev, as it is. You can also run it locally (see [Run, test, build](#run-test-build): `npm ci`, then `npm run dev`; a local run has no AI endpoint, so free-text questions to the AI in step 6 do not work there; the sample-question buttons show their saved answers).

1. **Map of Japan.** It opens at 2050, the last year of the IPSS official projection. The first screen shows only what matters most: the title and the question, the map with the year and the legend, 「分かれ目」 (the turning points, item 7 below) and a row 「くわしく見る」 (see in detail). The panels for town details, assumptions, population allocation, asking the AI and the sources start folded; pressing a name in that row, or a panel's title, opens it. Prefectures are coloured, and a switch above the map chooses what the colour means. 「人口の変化」 (population change) is how much of the 2020 population remains: the paler, the fewer (nine steps of one indigo; the fewest are left white, with no colour; the same as 2020 is a dark indigo, areas that grew are the darkest), and the legend says how to read it. 「高齢者1人を支える人数」 (people aged 15-64 per person aged 65+) is darker, nearer black, where there are fewer of them and it is harder (six grey steps; the most are white). To see 2100, press the play button (▶) of the year slider at the bottom or move the slider; it runs from 2020 to 2100 in 5-year steps.
2. **Prefecture to town.** Click a prefecture to get its municipal map. You can search by town name. Designated cities and the 23 Tokyo wards are drawn ward by ward.
3. **Town panel.** Population over time (official projection as a solid line, this app's own calculation as a dashed line), a population pyramid by 5-year age group and sex, the shares aged 65+ and 0-14, the number of working-age people supporting one older person (高齢者1人を支える現役世代: people aged 15-64 divided by people aged 65+) and its trend, and for each facility a comparison with its "population guideline". There are 48 facility types; the 8 the app treats as essential come first and the rest are collapsed. The on-screen names of the 8 are 飲食料品小売業 (food retail), 郵便局 (post office), 銀行（中央銀行を除く） (banks, central bank excluded), 一般診療所 (general clinic), 介護老人福祉施設 (special nursing home), 一般病院 (general hospital), 通所・短期入所介護事業 (day care / short-stay care) and 訪問介護事業 (home help). Facility names on screen are the names in `public/data/facilities.json` (the AI writes its own wording, which can differ). Each facility shown as 「人口の目安を下回る」 (below the guideline) has a 「この目安に届くには」 ("to reach this guideline") disclosure: how many more people the town would need to reach the guideline, and, as a worked example, which other towns would fall below some facility's guideline if that many people moved in from elsewhere (not a prediction of anyone's moves; not shown for towns in the three metropolitan areas). 「地図に反映する」 draws that calculation on the map. The last block, 「いま、この規模の市町村は、1人あたりこれだけ使っています」 ("what municipalities of this size spend per resident today"), shows what municipalities as large as the town is in the selected year spent per resident in fiscal 2024 (total expenditure / registered residents): the median and the middle half, and the town's own figure. It compares actuals; it is not a forecast of what the town will spend.
4. **「前提を変える」 (Change the assumptions).** Moves the assumptions used after 2050: how easily children are born (0.6-1.4 times the 2050 level), migration (keep the trend around 2050, or fade it to zero by 2100; strength 0-1.5), and an infrastructure-linked acceleration of depopulation (none, weak, strong). The map and numbers change immediately. Under each bar and button group, the national population in 2100 at each position (this app's estimate, other assumptions at their defaults) is shown. Numbers up to 2050 never change.
5. **「人口の割り振り」 (Allocate population).** Keeps the national total fixed, increases (or decreases) the population of one prefecture or town, and shows how the rest change. To try it: (1) set the year slider to 2100; (2) in 「人口の割り振り」 choose Wakayama Prefecture in 「人口を増減させる地域」 (it is the starting choice when nothing is selected; the percentage starts at +10%); (3) press 「地図に反映する」 ("show on the map"). The map colours then show the change in population caused by the allocation. Raising Wakayama Prefecture by 10% (about 26,000 people) makes the other regions lose the same number in total. 高野町 (Koya) goes from 470.6 to 517.7 people and is no longer below the 500-person guideline. Eight facility types have 500 as their guideline (「飲食料品小売業」 food retail, 「飲食店」 restaurants, 「酒場、ビアホール」 bars, 「旅館、ホテル」 hotels, 「郵便局」 post offices, 「一般診療所」 clinics, 「歯科診療所」 dental clinics, 「介護老人福祉施設」 special nursing homes). That 500 is the midpoint of the source table's 0-1,000 class, not an observed step. Since the national difference is small, results are shown as the change in the number of towns that are no longer below, or that fall below, each facility's guideline (in this example 12 of the 48 facility types change). Pressing a facility name in the table marks the towns that crossed its guideline: on a prefecture map as rings (blue solid = no longer below the guideline, rust-red dashed = below the guideline), on the national map as one marker per prefecture ("+n −n"). To clear the allocation, press 「割り振りを地図から消す」 (shown while an allocation is on the map) or the Esc key (Esc does nothing while the focus is in a text field or a select).
6. **「AIに質問」 (Ask the AI).** Type a question in Japanese into the 「AIに質問」 panel and send it (up to 200 characters). The five sample-question buttons send a question in the same way. The real model (Claude Sonnet 5.5) calls the app's calculations as tools and answers from their results. Open 「使った道具と数字」 under an answer to see which tool was called with what and what it returned.

   What to expect:
   - An answer usually takes about 5 to 15 seconds (a short question comes back sooner). A question that uses tools makes 2 to 4 calls to the proxy (Worker); a question that uses no tool makes 1. Over the 95 questions checked, the proxy's response times added up to 2.9 to 12.2 seconds per question (median 5.6 seconds), and the screen adds network and calculation time. The two times seen while recording the demo video were about 9 seconds. While waiting, the panel shows 「計算の結果を引いています…」 ("fetching the calculation results").
   - The same question can get differently worded answers each time, because the model writes each one anew. The numbers in an answer are compared with the tool results (scope and limits: "How Claude is used"). The app's town figures up to 2050 can differ from the IPSS table by a few people, yet the AI may call them the official IPSS projection.
   - Saved answers (answers the real model gave to the sample questions beforehand, shown instantly without the proxy): there are five, and the author read them in full and approved them (6 November 2026, `verified`). Pressing a sample-question button (or sending the same sentence as the first question of a conversation) shows the saved answer at once, without calling the proxy, with a 「保存済みの回答」 ("saved answer") badge and the note 「あらかじめ保存した回答です…」 ("this is an answer saved beforehand…") (`public/data/cached_answers.json`). There is one known limit. The answer about 田辺市 calls the 2050 population of 42,311 the IPSS official projection, whereas the IPSS table says 42,309 and 42,311 is this app's calculation. It is written in the file's note. The answer about the Wakayama allocation was made again after the tool gained its list of the areas that lose people (the afternoon of 6 November 2026), and the author read and approved it.
   - When a limit is reached (60 calls per IP per day and 400 in total per day, counted in upstream calls; the 95 questions checked made 2.43 calls each on average (an average of the checking questions; one question can cost up to 9 calls), so on that average the 400 in total are about 165 questions and the 60 per IP about 25; plus at most 20 per 60 seconds per IP), or the proxy cannot be reached, the panel shows the question's saved answer if it has one, with the note 「いまはAIに接続できないため、保存済みの答えを表示しています。」 ("the AI cannot be reached now, so the saved answer is shown"). If it has none, the panel says 「いまはAIに接続できません。保存済みの質問を試してください。」 ("The AI cannot be reached right now. Please try a saved question.") and lists the five saved-question buttons.
   - A build that names no AI address, such as a local `npm run dev`, has a panel whose first sentence says 「このバージョンでは、AIへの質問は使えません（AIの接続先がまだ用意されていません）。地図、数値、前提の調整、人口の割り振りはそのまま使えます。」 ("AI questions are not available in this version (no AI endpoint has been set up yet). The map, the numbers, the assumptions and the population allocation work as usual."); the sample-question buttons show their saved answers, while a free-text question only gets a card with the same sentence ([docs/ai-endpoint.md](docs/ai-endpoint.md)). The public page was built with the AI address named.
7. **「分かれ目」 (the turning points).** At the top of the right column, for the place you chose (all Japan, a prefecture or a town): when, where and for which facility the population falls below the MLIT population guideline (the population at which half of the towns of that size have the facility; 2009-2012 statistics). It shows a strip of bars per 5 years (any of the 8 main facilities; solid up to 2050, dashed outline for the estimate after that) and rows in order of time (year, facility, town). Falling below does not mean the facility will close, and the app does not say what to keep. Opening 「ここを残すには」 (to keep this) on a row shows, as two bars of equal length, how many more people the town would need to reach the guideline and how many other towns would fall below a guideline if that many people moved in from the other towns in proportion to their population (a worked example, not a forecast of anyone's moves). With the default assumptions, of the 128 national rows only 3 make another town fall below a guideline (one town each, in 2060, an estimate), because the people needed are small next to the other towns' populations (taking them from the same prefecture instead gave about the same result). So on the other rows the app only says that the total does not change, so what one town gains the others lose. When a prefecture or a town is chosen, the same card also shows 「ここにある有名なものの例」 (famous places here): name, town, kind, one sentence and source. It is not a complete list and has nothing to do with whether the population falls below a guideline; it says nothing about whether any named place will remain, and the places or their operators did not take part and do not endorse the app.

Convention: every number after 2050 carries a 「試算」 (own calculation) badge and is drawn dashed. The app never calls it a forecast. Up to 2050 it says 「公式推計」 (official projection); 2020 says 「実績（国勢調査）」 (census). While an allocation is on the map, a figure up to 2050 that the allocation changed is no longer the official value but this app's own calculation, so the headline shows 「割り振り後」 and the note 「公式推計をもとにこのアプリが計算」 instead of 「公式推計」. AI answer cards follow the same rule: an answer that shows or names a year after 2050 (and, when in doubt, it is treated as naming one) carries the 「試算」 badge and the note 「2050年より後は、このアプリの試算です（予測ではありません）」.

The map can also be used from the keyboard. Tab enters a map at a single stop per map (a link 「地図をとばして、年の選択へ」, "skip the map, go to the year", appears in front of it). Arrow keys move to the neighbouring area in that direction, PageUp and PageDown to the previous and next area, Home and End to the first and last area, and Enter or Space select. With PageUp/PageDown every area can be reached, including those the arrows cannot reach (a test checks this on the national map and on all 47 prefecture maps).

## Where the numbers come from and how they are checked

**Base data:** the National Institute of Population and Social Security Research (IPSS), "Regional Population Projections for Japan (2023 estimate, reiwa 5)". It publishes municipal projections to 2050 from the 2020 census, and the assumptions behind them (survival rates, net migration rates, children-women ratio, sex ratio at 0-4) per municipality. There is no official municipal projection beyond 2050.

**This app's calculation** is its own cohort-component engine (5-year age groups, by sex, 5-year steps) driven by those assumptions. First, it was checked that the published assumptions reproduce the official 2050 results (1,871 municipalities with published assumptions):

| Year | Per-municipality population error: median | 90% of municipalities within | Max |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 0.0039% | 0.042% | 0.705% |
| 2030 | 0.0041% | 0.051% | 0.995% |
| 2040 | 0.0049% | 0.057% | 0.841% |
| 2050 | 0.0053% | 0.076% | 1.39% |

In 2050, 99.47% of municipalities are within 0.5%. The national total is 104,686,401 (this app) against 104,686,386 (official), over all 1,884 areas (the 13 villages without published assumptions use the official age-group populations as they are). The values in the table and the totals were measured again at commit ca909ba (the calculation engine has not changed since fa21f09). What the tests check on every run are thresholds for 2050 only (they fail unless the 2050 median error is within 0.01%, at least 98% of municipalities are within 0.5%, the max is within 2%, and the national total is within 1,000 of the official one). How this was found is in [docs/dev-log.md](docs/dev-log.md).

This app's numbers up to 2050 can differ from IPSS's published values by a few people for a town and a few tens for a prefecture (the tests check this on all 1,884 areas: under 10 for a town, under 100 for a prefecture). For example, 田辺市 (Tanabe) in 2050 is 42,309 in IPSS's table and 42,311 in this app's calculation. Opening 「人口の推移の数字を表で見る」 ("see the numbers as a table") in the town panel shows the same note under the table.

**After 2050**, by default the assumptions of the 2045-2050 period are held constant. The national 2100 total is 63,279,524 (63.28 million), close to IPSS's separately published long-range reference projection (2071-2120; medium fertility, medium mortality: 62.78 million in 2100). The assumptions can be changed on screen:

| National population in 2100 | This app's calculation |
|---|---|
| Assumptions held (default) | 63.28 million |
| Migration fades to zero by 2100 | 58.52 million |
| Children-women ratio x1.2 | 70.93 million |
| Children-women ratio x0.8 | 56.34 million |
| Fade and x1.2 | 65.06 million |

When migration stops, the national total falls, but small shrinking towns can end up with more people because their net out-migration also stops (Tanabe City goes from 11,880 to 13,690 in 2100).

**The infrastructure-linked acceleration of depopulation** is an assumption added by this app. The official assumptions do not tie migration to the presence of hospitals or shops. This assumption says that when an essential facility falls below its population guideline, more people leave. There is no empirical basis for how many more; it is only there to show a range, and it is off by default.

**Facility "population guidelines"** come from the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT) table of the population size at which 50% (p50) and 80% (p80) of municipalities have at least one such establishment, from 2009-2012 statistics, excluding the three major metropolitan areas (11 prefectures: Saitama, Chiba, Tokyo, Kanagawa, Gifu, Aichi, Mie, Kyoto, Osaka, Hyogo, Nara). For each facility the screen shows a town as 「人口の目安を上回る」 (above the guideline: at or above the 80% value), 「境目」 (borderline: from the 50% value up to the 80% value) or 「人口の目安を下回る」 (below the guideline: under the 50% value); a facility whose 50% and 80% values are equal has no borderline band. It is a guideline, not a prediction that a facility will close. Towns in the three metropolitan areas are marked as outside the table's scope and shown for reference.

### Limits of the numbers

- After 2050 it is a calculation under stated assumptions, not a forecast. Small towns depend more on the assumptions.
- The facility guidelines are 2009-2012 statistics; at the same population they do not match today's facilities.
- 13 villages have no published assumptions (檜枝岐村, 利島村, 御蔵島村, 青ヶ島村, 粟島浦村, 平谷村, 野迫川村, 下北山村, 上北山村, 北山村, 三島村, 座間味村, 渡名喜村). They use official values up to 2050 and then follow their prefecture's rate of change. This version has no on-screen marker for them yet.
- The base is the 2020 census and the municipal areas of around 2020; later mergers are not reflected.
- Map boundaries are coarse shapes simplified for display (not for surveying, area or administrative checks); coastlines and borders can be off by tens to hundreds of metres or more, and tiny rocks under 0.01 km2 are mostly dropped.
- The six villages of the Northern Territories (色丹村, 泊村, 留夜別村, 留別村, 紗那村, 蘂取村) are not drawn because IPSS has no figures for them; this is not a statement about territory. The Habomai Islands are included in 根室市 in the source data and so are in its shape.

**In the product** (it works on the public page; this describes the design and what the tests and the real model confirmed): the 「AIに質問」 panel calls the Anthropic API (model `claude-sonnet-5-5`) through a small Cloudflare Worker. The API key is kept only as a Worker secret and is not in the repository or in the browser (when it was created, the browser's temporary files for the creation page briefly held its text and were deleted; see section 6.2 of [docs/dev-log.md](docs/dev-log.md)).

- Claude answers by calling 9 calculation tools. The tool names, descriptions and input shapes are fixed on the Worker's side; values sent from the browser are not used.
  1. `find_area`: find an area's code from its name
  2. `get_series`: an area's population over time (including the 65+ count and share)
  3. `compare_sources`: official projection against this app's calculation
  4. `run_projection`: recalculate under changed assumptions
  5. `facility_check`: compare a town's population with the facility guidelines
  6. `run_allocation`: calculate a population reallocation (the areas with the biggest change, the 10 areas that lose the most people with a summary, and the facility guidelines that are crossed)
  7. `find_similar_now`: find towns whose current population is close to a place's projected 2100 population
  8. `count_areas`: count areas that meet a condition
  9. `get_age_groups`: an area's population aged 0-14, 15-64 and 65 and over, with shares, year by year
- The tools run the same calculation engine inside the browser. Claude explains the results in Japanese.
- **The numbers in an answer are checked for whether they appear in the calculation results** (a small error is allowed: the larger of half a unit of the last digit shown and 0.5% of the value). The check only looks for the same number somewhere in the tool results of that question, or in the tool results of earlier questions of the same conversation; a number that occurs only in an earlier answer's text or in a question's text does not count as verified. It does not check that the number belongs to the right town or year, or that the explanation is correct. Also, the app's town figures up to 2050 come from its recomputation of the official method and can differ from the IPSS table by a few people (田辺市 in 2050: 42,311 here, 42,309 in the IPSS table); the tools return them in the "official" frame, so the AI may call them the official IPSS projection. The note under the table on the screen says the same. If a number is not found, the model is asked once to restate the answer using only numbers from the results. An answer that still has unverified numbers is shown with a warning (「次の数字は、計算の結果で確かめられませんでした」). Sums or ratios the model computes itself (for example "about 28%") are not in the tool results and are flagged. These are not checked: list numbers at the start of a line ("1.", "(3)"), digits right after a letter ("p50"), digits after 第, plain integers followed by 年 歳 代 位 番 月 日 章 節 号 条 ("2050年", "65歳以上", "11月5日"), 4-digit integers from 1900 to 2100 (years) that have no counter such as 人 世帯 戸 円 件 軒 台, 2- or 5-digit integers equal to an area code that appears in the results (when no such counter follows), integers 0-10 with no unit, %, 倍/割, decimal point or thousands comma, and dates such as "2026/11/05". Kanji numerals (for example 三万人) are not read.
- Humans can check the calculation: 「使った道具と数字」 opens under each answer. For allocation questions the map can be moved to the same state.
- The model's output is rendered as plain text only, never parsed as HTML.
- How the Worker protects itself: `messages` from the browser are rebuilt from checked fields (the one exception is the `input` of a `tool_use` block: any JSON object is passed on as received, and only counted in the total text limit); the upstream request is built only from constants, those `messages` and the tool definitions, redirects are not followed, every response carries `nosniff`, an unexpected failure becomes a 500 of one fixed shape, and the Worker logs only fixed strings (Workers Logs; no client IP or API key). Cloudflare may attach its own metadata to a recorded line, which cannot be checked outside the real environment; that is unverified, and opening a recorded line to look (the last part of step 9 of the deploy checklist in [worker/README.md](worker/README.md)) has not been done yet. The daily counting fails closed: when a counter cannot be read or cannot be saved, the request is refused and the upstream API is not called. So on Cloudflare's Free plan, once the KV write quota (1,000 writes a day for the account) is used up, the AI stops until 00:00 UTC the next day (a Paid plan does not stop; which Cloudflare plan the deployed Worker is on has not been confirmed); and a request that writes to the same counter within a second of another is refused too, so the plain "the AI is not available right now" appears now and then. Details in [worker/README.md](worker/README.md).
- The Worker gives the model rules such as: do not recommend what to keep, do not call anything a forecast, do not write that a facility "cannot be kept". It also gives a rule that the screen shows the model's output as plain text, so no tables (the vertical bar), no bold or italic marks and no heading marks, and bullets only as "- " at the start of a line, not nested (added on 6 November 2026; before it, the model sometimes used tables and bold, whose marks would have shown as they are).

## Run, test, build

Requirements: Node.js and npm (developed on Node 26). To rebuild the data, Python 3 (standard library only; developed on 3.14).

```sh
npm ci              # install dependencies
npm run dev         # dev server (http://localhost:5173)
npm test            # tests (Vitest)
npm run typecheck   # type check (app and Worker)
npm run build       # build the site into dist/ (no source maps)
npm run test:py     # data pipeline tests (same as python3 -m unittest; run python3 -m pipeline.fetch_sources first)
```

Checked at commit ca909ba (2026-11-06):

- `npm test`: 1270 tests in 50 files, all pass
- `npm run typecheck` (app and Worker) and `npm run build`: no errors or warnings; no source map (`.map`) is written to `dist/`
- `python3 -m unittest`: with `data/raw` present (fetch it with `python3 -m pipeline.fetch_sources`) all 122 tests pass; without it 101 tests run and the 4 test classes that need the data stop with a "file is missing" error

Rebuilding the data (the results are committed in `public/data`, so this is normally unnecessary):

```sh
python3 -m pipeline.fetch_sources     # download the IPSS Excel files into data/raw and verify SHA256
python3 -m pipeline.build_areas       # per-area population and assumptions JSON (public/data/areas, groups.json, index.json)
python3 -m pipeline.build_facilities  # facility guidelines (public/data/facilities.json)
bash pipeline/build_geometry.sh       # boundary TopoJSON (public/data/geo); downloads the N03 zips and simplifies
python3 -m pipeline.check_codes       # cross-check IPSS area codes against the boundaries
```

The AI proxy (Worker): see [worker/README.md](worker/README.md) for its behaviour and settings and [docs/ai-endpoint.md](docs/ai-endpoint.md) for how the page finds it. The public page is the build made with `VITE_CHAT_ENDPOINT=/api/chat`, placed in the Cloudflare Pages project `japan2100`. The page's `/api/chat` (`functions/api/chat.ts`) hands each call, through a service binding, to the Worker (`town-2100-api`) published on 6 November 2026. The Worker has no public address (workers.dev) of its own. To set up the same thing in your own account you need an Anthropic API key (create a dedicated workspace and set its spend limit first), a Cloudflare KV namespace and Rate Limiting binding, and the Worker's secrets registered; the steps are the checklist in worker/README.md (the key is never written in code or config files). If the page's `/api/chat` calls the Worker, build with `VITE_CHAT_ENDPOINT=/api/chat` as well: then the first sentence of the AI panel is the one for a working AI from the start (without it the build counts as naming no address and says first that questions are not available).
