# Japan in 2100 (town-2100)

[日本語](README.md)

A map that shows Japan's population up to 2100. You drill down from Japan to a prefecture to a town and see each town's population, its age structure, and whether that population is above or below each facility's "population guideline". You can also reallocate people between regions and see what changes. An AI calls the app's own calculations as tools and answers from their results.

The app does not recommend what to keep or give up. It shows facts so people can decide.

The author's view (作者の見解): the author thinks that if every rural region in Japan were revitalised at the same time, it would not add up in terms of population. The app is not a proof of that view; it lays out material for checking it. (This sentence was written by Claude from the author's remarks; its wording is awaiting the author's confirmation.)

- Try it: https://japan2100.pages.dev (Cloudflare Pages; set so that search engines do not index it). Questions to the AI go through the proxy (a Cloudflare Worker, https://town-2100-api.your-account.workers.dev ) to the real model (Claude Sonnet 5.5). AI calls are limited (60 per visitor per day and 400 in total per day, counted in upstream calls; the questions checked used 1 to 4 calls each; the day is the UTC date, which turns over at 09:00 Japan time), and the proxy's API key expires on 6 December 2026.

## Try it in 3 minutes (for judges)

Open the public page, https://japan2100.pages.dev, as it is. You can also run it locally (see [Run, test, build](#run-test-build): `npm ci`, then `npm run dev`; a local run has no AI endpoint, so step 6, the AI, does not work there).

1. **Map of Japan.** It opens at 2050, the last year of the IPSS official projection. Prefectures are coloured by change relative to 2020 (decline in rust red, growth in blue). To see 2100, press the play button (▶) of the year slider at the bottom or move the slider; it runs from 2020 to 2100 in 5-year steps.
2. **Prefecture to town.** Click a prefecture to get its municipal map. You can search by town name. Designated cities and the 23 Tokyo wards are drawn ward by ward.
3. **Town panel.** Population over time (official projection as a solid line, this app's own calculation as a dashed line), a population pyramid by 5-year age group and sex, the shares aged 65+ and 0-14, and for each facility a comparison with its "population guideline". There are 48 facility types; the 8 the app treats as essential come first and the rest are collapsed. The on-screen names of the 8 are 飲食料品小売業 (food retail), 郵便局 (post office), 銀行（中央銀行を除く） (banks, central bank excluded), 一般診療所 (general clinic), 介護老人福祉施設 (special nursing home), 一般病院 (general hospital), 通所・短期入所介護事業 (day care / short-stay care) and 訪問介護事業 (home help). Facility names on screen are the names in `public/data/facilities.json` (the AI writes its own wording, which can differ).
4. **「前提を変える」 (Change the assumptions).** Moves the assumptions used after 2050: how easily children are born (0.6-1.4 times the 2050 level), migration (keep the trend around 2050, or fade it to zero by 2100; strength 0-1.5), and an infrastructure-linked acceleration of depopulation (none, weak, strong). The map and numbers change immediately. Numbers up to 2050 never change.
5. **「人口の割り振り」 (Allocate population).** Keeps the national total fixed, increases (or decreases) the population of one prefecture or town, and shows how the rest change. To try it: (1) set the year slider to 2100; (2) in 「人口の割り振り」 choose Wakayama Prefecture in 「人口を増減させる地域」 (it is the starting choice when nothing is selected; the percentage starts at +10%); (3) press 「地図に反映する」 ("show on the map"). The map colours then show the change in population caused by the allocation. Raising Wakayama Prefecture by 10% (about 26,000 people) makes the other regions lose the same number in total. 高野町 (Koya) goes from 470.6 to 517.7 people and is no longer below the 500-person guideline. Eight facility types have 500 as their guideline (「飲食料品小売業」 food retail, 「飲食店」 restaurants, 「酒場、ビアホール」 bars, 「旅館、ホテル」 hotels, 「郵便局」 post offices, 「一般診療所」 clinics, 「歯科診療所」 dental clinics, 「介護老人福祉施設」 special nursing homes). That 500 is the midpoint of the source table's 0-1,000 class, not an observed step. Since the national difference is small, results are shown as the change in the number of towns that are no longer below, or that fall below, each facility's guideline (in this example 12 of the 48 facility types change). Pressing a facility name in the table marks the towns that crossed its guideline: on a prefecture map as rings (blue solid = no longer below the guideline, rust-red dashed = below the guideline), on the national map as one marker per prefecture ("+n −n"). To clear the allocation, press 「割り振りを地図から消す」 (shown while an allocation is on the map) or the Esc key (Esc does nothing while the focus is in a text field or a select).
6. **「AIに質問」 (Ask the AI).** Type a question in Japanese into the 「AIに質問」 panel and send it (up to 200 characters). The five sample-question buttons send a question in the same way. The real model (Claude Sonnet 5.5) calls the app's calculations as tools and answers from their results. Open 「使った道具と数字」 under an answer to see which tool was called with what and what it returned.

   What to expect:
   - The first answer takes about 5 to 15 seconds. A question that uses tools makes 1 to 4 calls to the proxy (Worker). Over the 95 questions checked, the proxy's response times added up to 2.9 to 12.2 seconds per question (median 5.6 seconds), and the screen adds network and calculation time. The two times seen while recording the demo video were about 9 seconds. While waiting, the panel shows 「計算の結果を引いています…」 ("fetching the calculation results").
   - The same question can get differently worded answers each time, because the model writes each one anew. The numbers in an answer are compared with the tool results (scope and limits: "How Claude is used").
   - Saved answers (the sample questions with answers the author has checked, shown instantly without the proxy): five were made, but all are still awaiting the author's check (`pending`). Until they are checked they are not shown, and the 「保存済みの質問」 buttons are not shown either (`public/data/cached_answers.json`; the file on the public page holds them too, but the screen does not show a `pending` answer).
   - When a limit is reached (60 calls per IP per day and 400 in total per day, counted in upstream calls; one question can cost up to 9 calls, so 60 is not always 60 questions; plus at most 20 per 60 seconds per IP), or the proxy cannot be reached, the panel says 「いまはAIに接続できません。時間をおいて、もう一度お試しください。」 ("The AI cannot be reached right now. Please try again later."). If the question has a verified saved answer, that is shown instead (there is none now).
   - A build that names no AI address, such as a local `npm run dev`, has a panel whose first sentence says 「このバージョンでは、AIへの質問は使えません（AIの接続先がまだ用意されていません）。地図、数値、前提の調整、人口の割り振りはそのまま使えます。」 ("AI questions are not available in this version (no AI endpoint has been set up yet). The map, the numbers, the assumptions and the population allocation work as usual."); a question there only gets a card with the same sentence ([docs/ai-endpoint.md](docs/ai-endpoint.md)). The public page was built with the AI address named.

Convention: every number after 2050 carries a 「試算」 (own calculation) badge and is drawn dashed. The app never calls it a forecast. Up to 2050 it says 「公式推計」 (official projection); 2020 says 「実績（国勢調査）」 (census). While an allocation is on the map, a figure up to 2050 that the allocation changed is no longer the official value but this app's own calculation, so the headline shows 「割り振り後」 and the note 「公式推計をもとにこのアプリが計算」 instead of 「公式推計」. AI answer cards follow the same rule: an answer that shows or names a year after 2050 (and, when in doubt, it is treated as naming one) carries the 「試算」 badge and the note 「2050年より後は、このアプリの試算です（予測ではありません）」.

The map can also be used from the keyboard. Tab enters a map at a single stop per map (a link 「地図をとばして、年の選択へ」, "skip the map, go to the year", appears in front of it). Arrow keys move to the neighbouring area in that direction, PageUp and PageDown to the previous and next area, Home and End to the first and last area, and Enter or Space select. With PageUp/PageDown every area can be reached, including those the arrows cannot reach (a test checks this on the national map and on all 47 prefecture maps).

## Where the numbers come from and how they are checked

**Base data:** the National Institute of Population and Social Security Research (IPSS), "Regional Population Projections for Japan (2023 estimate, reiwa 5)". It publishes municipal projections to 2050 from the 2020 census, and the assumptions behind them (survival rates, net migration rates, children-women ratio, sex ratio at 0-4) per municipality. There is no official municipal projection beyond 2050.

**This app's calculation** is its own cohort-component engine (5-year age groups, by sex, 5-year steps) driven by those assumptions. First, it was checked that the published assumptions reproduce the official 2050 results (1,871 municipalities with published assumptions):

| Year | Per-municipality population error: median | 90% of municipalities within | Max |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 0.0039% | 0.042% | 0.705% |
| 2030 | 0.0041% | 0.051% | 0.995% |
| 2040 | 0.0049% | 0.057% | 0.841% |
| 2050 | 0.0053% | 0.076% | 1.39% |

In 2050, 99.47% of municipalities are within 0.5%. The national total is 104,686,401 (this app) against 104,686,386 (official), over all 1,884 areas (the 13 villages without published assumptions use the official age-group populations as they are). The values in the table and the totals were measured again at commit a15e934 (the calculation engine has not changed since fa21f09). What the tests check on every run are thresholds for 2050 only (they fail unless the 2050 median error is within 0.01%, at least 98% of municipalities are within 0.5%, the max is within 2%, and the national total is within 1,000 of the official one). How this was found is in [docs/dev-log.md](docs/dev-log.md).

This app's numbers up to 2050 can differ from IPSS's published values by a few people for a town and a few tens for a prefecture (the tests check this on all 1,884 areas: under 10 for a town, under 100 for a prefecture). For example, 田辺市 (Tanabe) in 2050 is 42,309 in IPSS's table and 42,311 in this app's calculation. Opening 「人口の推移の数字を表で見る」 ("see the numbers as a table") in the town panel shows the same note under the table.

**After 2050**, by default the assumptions of the 2045-2050 period are held constant. The national 2100 total is 63,279,524 (63.28 million), close to IPSS's separately published long-range reference projection (2071-2120; medium fertility, medium mortality: 62.78 million in 2100). The assumptions can be changed on screen:

| National population in 2100 | This app's calculation |
|---|---|
| Assumptions held (default) | 63.28 million |
| Migration fades to zero by 2100 | 58.52 million |
| Children-women ratio x1.2 | 70.93 million |
| Children-women ratio x0.8 | 56.34 million |
| Fade and x1.2 | 65.06 million |

When migration stops, the national total falls, but small shrinking towns can end up with more people because their net out-migration also stops (Tanabe City goes from 11,880 to 13,690 in 2100).

**The infrastructure-linked acceleration of depopulation** is an assumption added by this app. The official assumptions do not tie migration to the presence of hospitals or shops. This assumption says that when an essential facility falls below its population guideline, more people leave. There is no empirical basis for how many more; it is only there to show a range, and it is off by default.

**Facility "population guidelines"** come from the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT) table of the population size at which 50% (p50) and 80% (p80) of municipalities have at least one such establishment, from 2009-2012 statistics, excluding the three major metropolitan areas (11 prefectures: Saitama, Chiba, Tokyo, Kanagawa, Gifu, Aichi, Mie, Kyoto, Osaka, Hyogo, Nara). For each facility the screen shows a town as 「人口の目安を上回る」 (above the guideline: at or above the 80% value), 「境目」 (borderline: from the 50% value up to the 80% value) or 「人口の目安を下回る」 (below the guideline: under the 50% value); a facility whose 50% and 80% values are equal has no borderline band. It is a guideline, not a prediction that a facility will close. Towns in the three metropolitan areas are marked as outside the table's scope and shown for reference.

### Limits of the numbers

- After 2050 it is a calculation under stated assumptions, not a forecast. Small towns depend more on the assumptions.
- The facility guidelines are 2009-2012 statistics; at the same population they do not match today's facilities.
- 13 villages have no published assumptions (檜枝岐村, 利島村, 御蔵島村, 青ヶ島村, 粟島浦村, 平谷村, 野迫川村, 下北山村, 上北山村, 北山村, 三島村, 座間味村, 渡名喜村). They use official values up to 2050 and then follow their prefecture's rate of change. This version has no on-screen marker for them yet.
- The base is the 2020 census and the municipal areas of around 2020; later mergers are not reflected.
- Map boundaries are coarse shapes simplified for display (not for surveying, area or administrative checks); coastlines and borders can be off by tens to hundreds of metres or more, and tiny rocks under 0.01 km2 are mostly dropped.
- The six villages of the Northern Territories (色丹村, 泊村, 留夜別村, 留別村, 紗那村, 蘂取村) are not drawn because IPSS has no figures for them; this is not a statement about territory. The Habomai Islands are included in 根室市 in the source data and so are in its shape.

**In the product** (it works on the public page; this describes the design and what the tests and the real model confirmed): the 「AIに質問」 panel calls the Anthropic API (model `claude-sonnet-5-5`) through a small Cloudflare Worker. The API key is kept only as a Worker secret and is not in the repository or in the browser.

- Claude answers by calling 8 calculation tools. The tool names, descriptions and input shapes are fixed on the Worker's side; values sent from the browser are not used.
  1. `find_area`: find an area's code from its name
  2. `get_series`: an area's population over time (including the 65+ count and share)
  3. `compare_sources`: official projection against this app's calculation
  4. `run_projection`: recalculate under changed assumptions
  5. `facility_check`: compare a town's population with the facility guidelines
  6. `run_allocation`: calculate a population reallocation
  7. `find_similar_now`: find towns whose current population is close to a place's projected 2100 population
  8. `count_areas`: count areas that meet a condition
- The tools run the same calculation engine inside the browser. Claude explains the results in Japanese.
- **The numbers in an answer are checked for whether they appear in the calculation results** (a small error is allowed: the larger of half a unit of the last digit shown and 0.5% of the value). The check only looks for the same number somewhere in the tool results of that question, or in the tool results of earlier questions of the same conversation; a number that occurs only in an earlier answer's text or in a question's text does not count as verified. It does not check that the number belongs to the right town or year, or that the explanation is correct. If a number is not found, the model is asked once to restate the answer using only numbers from the results. An answer that still has unverified numbers is shown with a warning (「次の数字は、計算の結果で確かめられませんでした」). Sums or ratios the model computes itself (for example "about 28%") are not in the tool results and are flagged. These are not checked: list numbers at the start of a line ("1.", "(3)"), digits right after a letter ("p50"), digits after 第, plain integers followed by 年 歳 代 位 番 月 日 章 節 号 条 ("2050年", "65歳以上", "11月5日"), 4-digit integers from 1900 to 2100 (years) that have no counter such as 人 世帯 戸 円 件 軒 台, 2- or 5-digit integers equal to an area code that appears in the results (when no such counter follows), integers 0-10 with no unit, %, 倍/割, decimal point or thousands comma, and dates such as "2026/11/05". Kanji numerals (for example 三万人) are not read.
- Humans can check the calculation: 「使った道具と数字」 opens under each answer. For allocation questions the map can be moved to the same state.
- The model's output is rendered as plain text only, never parsed as HTML.
- How the Worker protects itself: `messages` from the browser are rebuilt from checked fields (the one exception is the `input` of a `tool_use` block: any JSON object is passed on as received, and only counted in the total text limit); the upstream request is built only from constants, those `messages` and the tool definitions, redirects are not followed, every response carries `nosniff`, an unexpected failure becomes a 500 of one fixed shape, and the Worker logs only fixed strings (Workers Logs; no client IP or API key). Cloudflare may attach its own metadata to a recorded line, which cannot be checked outside the real environment; that is unverified, and opening a recorded line to look (the last part of step 9 of the deploy checklist in [worker/README.md](worker/README.md)) has not been done yet. The daily counting fails closed: when a counter cannot be read or cannot be saved, the request is refused and the upstream API is not called. So on Cloudflare's Free plan, once the KV write quota (1,000 writes a day for the account) is used up, the AI stops until 00:00 UTC the next day (a Paid plan does not stop; which Cloudflare plan the deployed Worker is on has not been confirmed); and a request that writes to the same counter within a second of another is refused too, so the plain "the AI is not available right now" appears now and then. Details in [worker/README.md](worker/README.md).
- The Worker gives the model rules such as: do not recommend what to keep, do not call anything a forecast, do not write that a facility "cannot be kept".

## Run, test, build

Requirements: Node.js and npm (developed on Node 26). To rebuild the data, Python 3 (standard library only; developed on 3.14).

```sh
npm ci              # install dependencies
npm run dev         # dev server (http://localhost:5173)
npm test            # tests (Vitest)
npm run typecheck   # type check (app and Worker)
npm run build       # build the site into dist/ (no source maps)
npm run test:py     # data pipeline tests (same as python3 -m unittest; run python3 -m pipeline.fetch_sources first)
```

Checked at commit a15e934 (2026-11-06):

- `npm test`: 1270 tests in 50 files, all pass
- `npm run typecheck` (app and Worker) and `npm run build`: no errors or warnings; no source map (`.map`) is written to `dist/`
- `python3 -m unittest`: with `data/raw` present (fetch it with `python3 -m pipeline.fetch_sources`) all 122 tests pass; without it 101 tests run and the 4 test classes that need the data stop with a "file is missing" error

Rebuilding the data (the results are committed in `public/data`, so this is normally unnecessary):

```sh
python3 -m pipeline.fetch_sources     # download the IPSS Excel files into data/raw and verify SHA256
python3 -m pipeline.build_areas       # per-area population and assumptions JSON (public/data/areas, groups.json, index.json)
python3 -m pipeline.build_facilities  # facility guidelines (public/data/facilities.json)
bash pipeline/build_geometry.sh       # boundary TopoJSON (public/data/geo); downloads the N03 zips and simplifies
python3 -m pipeline.check_codes       # cross-check IPSS area codes against the boundaries
```

The AI proxy (Worker): see [worker/README.md](worker/README.md) for its behaviour and settings and [docs/ai-endpoint.md](docs/ai-endpoint.md) for how the page finds it. The public page is the build made with `VITE_CHAT_ENDPOINT=https://town-2100-api.your-account.workers.dev/api/chat`, naming the Worker (`town-2100-api`) published on 6 November 2026, placed in the Cloudflare Pages project `japan2100`. To set up the same thing in your own account you need an Anthropic API key (create a dedicated workspace and set its spend limit first), a Cloudflare KV namespace and Rate Limiting binding, and the Worker's secrets registered; the steps are the checklist in worker/README.md (the key is never written in code or config files). If the Worker is routed on the same origin as the page, build with `VITE_CHAT_ENDPOINT=/api/chat` as well: then the first sentence of the AI panel is the one for a working AI from the start (without it the build counts as naming no address and says first that questions are not available).

## Limits and unfinished items

- **What is published and what is not.** The page (https://japan2100.pages.dev) and the AI proxy (Worker) have been published since 6 November 2026. Not published: the repository itself (private until the submission) and the place where the demo video is kept (it is linked from the submission form). The five saved AI answers are all still awaiting the author's check (`pending`) and are not shown on screen; the five unchecked entries are in the file the public page serves (`cached_answers.json`).
- **What was checked against the real API (6 November 2026).** The published Worker was called with the same `ask()` the page uses, through a script (`scripts/ai_live_check.ts`; how it runs and the breakdown are in [docs/ai-live-check.md](docs/ai-live-check.md)). There were 235 calls to the Worker in all, every one HTTP 200 (no 400, 429, 502 or 503). The API accepted the request shape the Worker sends (adaptive thinking with `block_binding` and the beta header, `effort` low, `max_tokens` 3000, `cache_control` on the system prompt). All 8 tool definitions were accepted and the model called all 8. The tool round trip (`tool_use`, `tool_result`, answer) works. Two 12-question conversations, each trimming old exchanges on 4 of its questions, produced no 400. `input_transformations` was empty (`[]`) before old exchanges were dropped and was only `thinking_dropped` (reason `prefix_binding_mismatch`) after. 233 of the 235 calls read the cache (the fixed part with the tools and system prompt is 5,941 tokens, 5,989 after the redeploy; the other 2 calls wrote it). `stop_reason` was only `tool_use` and `end_turn` (no `max_tokens`, no `refusal`); the largest output was 578 tokens. A one-line question took 6,023 input tokens and a 20,000-character question 23,302 (0.86 per character). One Worker response took a median of 2.3 seconds and at most 5.9 seconds, measured from this Mac. The cost, estimated from the usage figures, was about 1.80 dollars (after the first 161 calls the Console balance had fallen by about 1.17 dollars, against an estimate of 1.18).
- **What was not tried against the real API.** The refusal (`refusal`) path (unit tests only); the 429 when a daily limit is hit (unit tests only; it needs a redeploy that lowers the limit, which was skipped); the retry on 429 and 503 (it never happened live); the cache after a gap of more than 5 minutes; many people at once and the counting across several locations; whether a line actually recorded in Workers Logs carries no client IP or API key; and whether Cloudflare's Workers plan is Free or Paid.
- **The Cloudflare plan is not confirmed. On the Free plan the AI would stop after about 500 calls a day.** When the KV write quota (1,000 a day on the Free plan; each counted call writes twice) is used up, the AI stops until 00:00 UTC (09:00 Japan time) and the panel says 「いまはAIに接続できません。時間をおいて、もう一度お試しください。」. On a Paid plan it does not stop. Checking the plan waits for the author's decision.
- **The cost estimate is not a guarantee.** The Worker's estimate is about 0.17 dollars for a worst single call, and about 68 dollars as the most that 400 counted calls (the daily limit) can cost (computed with the fixed part at the estimated 4,600 tokens; recomputed with the measured 5,941 tokens: about 0.173 and about 69 dollars). The measured average was about 0.008 dollars per call (about 1.80 dollars for 235 calls). But the daily count is a courtesy mechanism: concurrent requests all pass the check together. A request whose counters cannot be saved is refused, which keeps the overshoot small (in the model of the table in [worker/README.md](worker/README.md): at most 15 calls per wave, and at most 415 calls, about 71 dollars, in a day), but that model has one location only and the overshoot with several locations is not measured, so it is not a guarantee. The only reliable stop on spending is on the Anthropic side: a dedicated workspace for this Worker with a monthly spend limit (100 dollars), an e-mail alert at 25 dollars, and per-minute limits (for Claude Sonnet 5.5: 120 requests, 400,000 input tokens and 150,000 output tokens). The API key expires on 6 December 2026.
- Whether the 測量法 approval line on the N03 boundary data must be shown on derived data is unconfirmed ([docs/sources.md](docs/sources.md), open items). The permission of the original provider (the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan) is confirmed only as far as MLIT states it in its terms. The IPSS terms page could not be re-read when the sources were consolidated. The page is published with these still unconfirmed; the author does the confirming.

Every review of the Worker code was also by Claude (the per-task reviews, a security review and a whole-branch review by Claude Opus 5.5, and Claude Code's automated security check). The author decided that the Worker could be published in this state (6 November 2026). The only reliable stop on spending is the limit on the Anthropic side.
